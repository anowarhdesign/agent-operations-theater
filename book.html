The Book of UI Math

Perfect-looking screens aren't luck. Every good layout follows a handful of small formulas. This book is those formulas, one chapter each, with a live demo you can play with. It is also the rulebook the Scene Engine follows when it reads a JSON recipe.

1Scale: sizes that belong together

Pick one base size and one ratio. Every other size is the base multiplied by the ratio again and again. Sizes made this way always look related.

size(n) = base × ratio ⁿ e.g. 16 × 1.25³ = 31.25px
Try it

2Rhythm: everything on a 4-point beat

All spacing and line heights are multiples of 4 (or 8). Like music in time, the eye feels the beat even when it can't see it.

space(k) = 4 × k → 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 line-height = round(size × 1.5 ÷ 4) × 4

3Grid: columns that always add up

Split the width into equal columns with equal gaps. Every block spans a whole number of columns, so edges line up everywhere on the page.

column = (width − 2 × margin − (n − 1) × gutter) ÷ n span(k) = k × column + (k − 1) × gutter
Try it

4Measure: lines you can actually read

Keep lines of text between 45 and 75 characters. Too long and the eye loses its place going back; too short and reading becomes choppy.

width ≈ characters × 0.5em 66 characters ≈ 33em ≈ 528px at 16px
Drag to change the line length

5Contrast: text everyone can see

Text needs enough brightness difference from its background. Normal text needs at least 4.5 : 1, large text 3 : 1. This is the WCAG formula.

L = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B (R, G, B linearized) ratio = (L_light + 0.05) ÷ (L_dark + 0.05)

6Motion: curves, not straight lines

Real things speed up and slow down. A cubic Bézier curve describes that change; most UI motion lasts 150 to 400 ms.

B(t) = (1−t)³P₀ + 3(1−t)²t P₁ + 3(1−t)t² P₂ + t³P₃ ease-out = (0, 0, 0.2, 1) linear = (0, 0, 1, 1)

7Depth: how a point cloud becomes a picture

A Game Boy screen was dots in X and Y, changing over time. A point cloud is dots in X, Y and Z, changing over time: XYZT. To draw it, divide each dot's X and Y by how far away it is. Far dots shrink toward the center; that is all perspective is.

x' = f × x ÷ z y' = f × y ÷ z (f = focal length)
2,000 dots, drawn only with this formula

8Light: why point clouds light so easily

Each dot can carry the direction its surface faces (its normal). Brightness is just how directly that normal points at the light. No baking, no textures: move the light and every dot updates.

brightness = max(0, N · L) (N = surface normal, L = light direction)
